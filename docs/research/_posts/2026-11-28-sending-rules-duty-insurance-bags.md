*As of 28 November 2026.*

The rules that shape what you can send from the US to Nigeria, and how: Nigerian duty, how air cargo is screened, what carriers ask for at drop-off, parcel insurance, and airline bag allowances.

## Nigerian duty

Small personal consignments under a set value are duty-free, up to four times a year. The whole order is assessed together, and splitting an order to stay under the limit can be penalised. Above the limit, duty plus several surcharges and VAT apply. The duty rate on phones is still unconfirmed. Some foods, used clothing, footwear and bags are on Nigeria's import prohibition list. The details are in the guide's pages on [Nigerian duty](../../guide/nigeria-duty.md) and [prohibited items](../../guide/prohibited-and-carry-warnings.md).

## Screening and export filing in the US

All cargo on passenger aircraft is screened to the same level as checked bags. Agents who accept cargo must check who the shipper is. Most personal shipments don't need a US export filing, but some items always do, and it is still unclear whether a sender-packed barrel counts as baggage or as household goods.

## At drop-off

Expect to give a full content list, photo ID and receipts for the goods. Some carriers reserve the right to open shipments, and a single banned item can hold up a whole container.

## Insurance

Off-the-shelf parcel insurance rarely covers Nigeria; several well-known insurers exclude it. Some forwarders offer their own optional cover. Read the actual policy wording before you rely on any of it.

## Airline bags

Free checked bags in economy from the US to Lagos vary by airline and fare, from none to two. Some airlines warn passengers not to carry items for anyone else, because the passenger is held responsible for what is inside. See the [carry-for-strangers warnings](../../guide/prohibited-and-carry-warnings.md) before you agree to carry a parcel.

## Still open

We have not yet read the customs law text itself, or confirmed several airlines' bag rules on their own pages. We also have not found a parcel insurer that covers Nigeria.
