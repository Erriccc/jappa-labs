# Nigeria import duty for personal shipments

Research date: **28 Nov 2026**. **This is not legal, customs or tax advice.** Rules change; confirm with the Nigeria Customs Service, or with the forwarder who will clear the goods.

Research behind this file: sending rules, https://github.com/Erriccc/jappa-labs/issues/16.

## The $300 duty-free rule (verified by two sources)

Sources:
- News Agency of Nigeria (NAN), **8 Sep 2025**, reprinted by Premium Times [fetched]: https://www.premiumtimesng.com/business/business-news/819441-customs-board-approves-300-duty-free-limit.html (the original NAN page returned 404).
- BusinessDay, **26 Sep 2025** [fetched]: https://businessday.ng/maritime/article/how-nigerias-300-duty-free-rule-affects-online-shoppers-international-travellers/

- **Under $300 is duty-free per consignment.** Each package is treated as a single consignment (BusinessDay, https://businessday.ng/maritime/article/how-nigerias-300-duty-free-rule-affects-online-shoppers-international-travellers/ ).
- **One duty-free import per quarter**, so four a year (NAN and BusinessDay). Unused quarters don't roll over (BusinessDay).
- **It does not cover prohibited or restricted goods** (NAN).
- **Penalties apply to invoice manipulation and undervaluing** (BusinessDay).
- **Splitting an order doesn't help.** There is only one duty-free import per quarter, so a second package in the same quarter pays duty anyway. Never suggest splitting an order.
- **It applies to e-commerce and to passenger baggage.** A relative carrying goods gets the same limit.
- **Commercial importers still pay.** The rule is for personal imports.

## Over $300

Duty runs **5–35%** depending on the item [research summary, 28 Nov 2026; URL not recorded]. On top of that come:
- a **7% surcharge** [URL not recorded];
- **4% FCS** [URL not recorded];
- **0.5% ETLS** [URL not recorded];
- **7.5% VAT** [URL not recorded].

Source: research summary, 28 Nov 2026; URL not recorded. How the layers stack (on which base each is charged) was not set out in the research. Don't present a computed total as exact; give a range and say it is an estimate.

## Phones and electronics

- **The duty rate on phones is unconfirmed**: somewhere in the 0–20% range [research summary; URL not recorded].
- **VAT alone is about $60 on an $800 phone** (7.5% of $800; rate above, URL not recorded).
- Through a forwarder that doesn't include duty (such as MyUS), duty and VAT on an $800 phone come to **at least $60–220** on top of shipping [arithmetic on the rates above; URL not recorded]. **This is a floor, not a total.** It counts only duty (0–20%) and VAT (7.5% of $800). It leaves out the 7% surcharge, 4% FCS and 0.5% ETLS, and VAT is normally charged on a base that already includes duty, so the real figure is higher. Present it as "at least", never as the full cost.
- Some Nigerian forwarders quote **flat all-in phone rates with duty included**: BigShipment /NG, new phone $70, laptop $80 [fetched, undated page, https://bigshipment.ng/NG/freight-shipping]. That is usually the simplest route for a phone.
- Hont's April 2026 PDF adds a **$30 handling charge on new gadgets** (used $20; five or more $100) [decoded, may be stale] https://www.hontglobal.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Hont-Global-Services-Shipping-Details-_April-2026.pdf .
- An $800 phone is **over the $300 limit even in a traveller's suitcase**.

## Larger and commercial shipments (secondary sources only)

The Nigeria Customs Service Act 2023 text was **not read**. Everything below is [snippet].
- **Barrels of personal effects** need no Form M. **Commercial shipments over $1,000** need Form M plus PAAR [URL not recorded].
- **Declarations:** a declarant can correct a declaration within 2 weeks (s.49(1), reported). The declarant is liable for false statements.
- **Penalty for a false statement:** ₦3,000,000 or up to 3 years in prison (section number unconfirmed).
- **Exam rate:** about 70% of goods were physically examined at Apapa in March 2026 [URL not recorded]. No airport exam rate was found.
- **Present at examination:** declarants reportedly have the right to be present (s.52(3)).

## US side: export filing (for large sends)

- US export filing (EEI/AES) is not needed for shipments of **$2,500 or less per Schedule B number** to one consignee (15 CFR 30.37(a)) [fetched; URL not recorded]. Household goods are reported (15 CFR 30.38) [fetched]. Baggage of people leaving the US is exempt (30.37(p)) [fetched].
- Whether a sender-packed barrel counts as baggage or as household goods is **unresolved**. Large barrel senders should ask the forwarder.
- The 15 CFR 30 sections were read in research, but their URLs were not recorded.
